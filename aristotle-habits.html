Aristotle & the Power of Habits

A story about small actions, steady practice, and the person we become.

01 / The story

A little, every day

This is an imagined teaching story inspired by Aristotle’s ideas about habit. The conversation is fictional, not a recorded historical event.

Long ago in ancient Greece, Aristotle taught his students that success was not the result of one great action, but of many small actions repeated over time.

One day, a young student asked him, “Master, how does a person become great?”

Aristotle pointed toward a nearby field where farmers worked every day.

“Do you see those farmers?” he asked.

The student nodded.

“They do not harvest their crops because they worked hard for one day. They harvest because they planted seeds, watered them, and cared for them day after day.”

The student thought for a moment and replied, “So greatness is like growing a crop?”

“Exactly,” said Aristotle. “A person becomes what they repeatedly do. If you practice honesty, you become honest. If you practice discipline, you become disciplined. If you practice kindness, you become kind.”

The student then asked, “What if I want to become successful?”

Aristotle smiled.

“Then do not focus only on success. Focus on the habits that create success. A single good decision can help you today, but good habits can help you for a lifetime.”

The student looked at the farmers again and finally understood.

Just as a harvest comes from many days of work, a good life comes from many days of good habits.

From that day forward, he paid less attention to grand achievements and more attention to the small actions he repeated every day.

Small actions, repeated over time, can make a lasting difference.
Vocabulary support
  • Habit: something you do regularly.
  • Harvest: to gather crops when they are ready.
  • Discipline: the ability to keep doing what is needed, even when it is difficult.
  • Repeatedly: again and again.
  • Achievement: something you accomplish through effort.
